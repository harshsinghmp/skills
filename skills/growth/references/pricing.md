# pricing — Pricing: value-based tiers, tested and aligned to positioning.

## Intake

- Packaging and features to price
- Customer willingness-to-pay signals and substitutes
- Competitor pricing and anchors
- Business model and margin targets

## Deliverable

A pricing recommendation: packaging/tiers, price points with rationale, the value metric (what you charge for), packaging fences, and a test plan — plus objection handling for the top price objections.

## Procedure

1. Establish the value metric (seat, usage, outcome) before the number.
2. Gather willingness signals: interviews, win/loss, substitutes, competitor anchors.
3. Choose the structure: good/better/best, usage-based, or flat — matched to the buyer.
4. Set price points anchored to value, not cost alone; use competitor prices as context, not truth.
5. Fence tiers so upgrades are natural (and don't cannibalize the top).
6. Handle the top objections (too expensive, free alternative, will churn) with value evidence.
7. Plan a test (new-cohort pricing, willingness surveys) and a review point.
8. Design pricing tests with 3-8 points per segment: map per-segment willingness-to-pay, plot revenue-max vs optimal spread, and anchor against competitive mapping.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Value metric defined before price.
- [ ] Willingness signals gathered, not assumed.
- [ ] Structure matched to the buyer.
- [ ] Prices anchored to value.
- [ ] Tiers fenced without cannibalization.
- [ ] Objection handling + test plan present.
- [ ] Test covers 3-8 points per segment with WTP map, revenue-max/optimal spread, and competitive anchor.
