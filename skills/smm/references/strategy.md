# strategy — Strategy: platform fit, positioning, content pillars, objectives.

## Intake

- Business goal social must serve (awareness, leads, community, retention)
- Audience: where they spend attention and who they trust
- Current channels, cadence, and performance baselines
- Capacity: who creates and publishes, how often
- Default stack: all 9 platforms in scope (Instagram, Facebook, TikTok, YouTube, LinkedIn, X, Pinterest, Snapchat, Threads); listening via Google Alerts + brand-watch monitoring. Fallback: ask which platforms/tools if the client uses others.

## Deliverable

A social strategy: chosen platforms with rationale, a positioning/mission line ('why follow'), 3–5 content pillars, cadence per platform, and objectives mapped to metrics.

## Procedure

1. Start from the business objective; social is a means, not the goal.
2. Choose platforms via the fit ladder — two done natively, expand only on evidence.
3. Define the 'why follow' line: the promise a follower gets for their attention.
4. Set 3–5 content pillars that rotate (educate, entertain, inspire, prove, invite).
5. Set cadence per platform from actual capacity — a sustainable rhythm, not a fantasy one.
6. Map each pillar to an objective and a metric; name what success looks like in 90 days.
7. Document the voice: tone, boundaries, and what the brand never posts.
8. Run localization QA in order — triage, translate/transcreate, adapt, compliance, format — and gate release on a quality score.
9. Triage listening signals with the intent rubric (source: marketingskills `social` SKILL.md): buying-intent and complaint signals get same-day response paths, category chatter gets weekly synthesis, noise gets muted — never treat all mentions equally. Seed the voice and pillars from a 500-post reverse-engineer of the niche's winners (hook + format + topic patterns tabled) before inventing net-new formats.
10. Ground the strategy in public-data research before inventing formats (source: ScrapeCreators `social-media-research-skills` — `outlier-post-finder`, `comment-mining`, `competitor-social-research`, MIT; workflows only, client's own scraping/API tooling supplies data): find outlier posts by lift vs each account's own median baseline per platform/format (huge 5x+, strong 2–5x, mild 1.5–2x; <10 posts = low confidence), explain winners by hook/format/topic/trigger; mine comments into questions, objections, complaints, requests, and buying intent, cluster themes by frequency × intensity with exact quotes preserved; tear down competitors (pillars, formats, hooks, cadence, CTAs, gaps) plus ad-library and trend sweeps. Never average baselines across platforms; never claim market sentiment from one post's comments.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Platforms chosen from the ladder, not 'everywhere'.
- [ ] A clear 'why follow' positioning line exists.
- [ ] 3–5 pillars, each mapped to an objective and metric.
- [ ] Cadence is sustainable for the stated team.
- [ ] Localization QA passed in order; quality-score gate met.
 - [ ] Voice and boundaries documented.
 - [ ] Listening triaged by intent (buying/complaint fast lane, chatter synthesized, noise muted); pillars seeded from a 500-post winner reverse-engineer.
 - [ ] Pillars checked against outlier/comment/competitor research (per-account median baselines, themed VOC quotes, named gaps).
- [ ] Voice drafts grounded in the client's own recent posts; quoted-source text kept out of the voice sample (keeper: mengto/write-like-meng-on-x).
- [ ] Draft options materially different in angle/structure, never surface paraphrases; no invented metrics or usage claims (keeper: mengto/write-like-meng-on-x).
- [ ] Bookmark-sourced quote posts state sample size and date range; thin history reported, not padded (keeper: mengto/x-bookmark-quote-posts).
- [ ] Profile shell audited per active platform: value-prop first line, specific niche, one proof element, CTA/link present, no hashtag stuffing in bios.
