# influencer — Influencer: creator fit, briefs, disclosure, and measurement.

## Intake

- Campaign goal and budget
- Target audience and creator niche/tier
- Deliverables (posts, stories, videos) and timeline
- Disclosure and usage-rights requirements
- Default stack: no scheduler assumption — creators post natively; amplification scheduled per client tooling. Fallback: ask how paid/whitelisted boosts are handled.

## Deliverable

An influencer plan: vetted creator shortlist with audience-fit rationale, a creative brief, contract essentials (deliverables, exclusivity, usage rights, disclosure), and a measurement plan with unique links/codes.

## Procedure

1. Define the goal (reach, conversions, UGC assets) before selecting creators.
2. Vet creators on audience fit and engagement quality, not follower count alone. Flag fakes: follower spikes without reach, comment pods, engagement far off tier norms. Match tier to use-case (nano/seeded trial, micro/conversion, macro/reach, hero/brand moment).
3. Write a brief that gives the hook and must-haves, then leaves creative freedom. Rank messaging must/should/may: must-say claims, should-show beats, may-skip options.
4. Set contract essentials: deliverables, dates, exclusivity, usage rights, disclosure.
5. Provide a unique link/code per creator for attribution.
6. Brief on FTC/ASA disclosure — it is mandatory, not optional.
7. Measure per creator: reach, engagement, traffic, conversions; report and repeat with winners.

## Quality gate

- [ ] Creators vetted for audience fit, not vanity size.
- [ ] Fake-follower flags checked; tier matches the use-case.
- [ ] Brief gives direction without killing the creator's voice.
- [ ] Must/should/may messaging hierarchy stated in the brief.
- [ ] Disclosure and usage rights covered in writing.
- [ ] Unique attribution per creator.
- [ ] Measurement tied to the campaign goal.
