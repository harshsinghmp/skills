# copy — High-Converting Copywriting Studio & Formula Engine

> **Operating Principle**: Copywriting formulas are not rigid fill-in-the-blank cages; they are battle-tested psychological rails proven across decades of direct-response marketing. They organize human emotion, curiosity, logic, and risk-reversal into an irresistible progression toward a single desired action.

---

## Intake & Context Discovery

Before choosing or executing any formula, discover and document:

1. **Target Reader & Current State**: Who specifically is reading? What is their current pain, frustration, or status quo?
2. **Awareness Level (Eugene Schwartz 5 Stages)**:
   - **Unaware**: Don't know they have a problem. Need broad curiosity, emotional storytelling, or provocative cultural hooks.
   - **Problem-Aware**: Feel the pain, but don't know solutions exist. Need empathy, agitation, and clear problem diagnosis.
   - **Solution-Aware**: Know solutions exist, but not your specific product. Need category education and differentiated mechanism.
   - **Product-Aware**: Know your product, but haven't committed. Need comparison, feature-benefit translation, proof, and risk reversal.
   - **Most Aware**: Ready to buy. Need the concrete offer, pricing clarity, bonuses, and deadline/CTA.
3. **Single Conversion Action**: What is the ONE measurable action this copy must compel? (e.g., Book demo, start trial, purchase, click link).
4. **Verified Product Truth**: What are the verified facts, benchmarks, proprietary mechanisms, and customer testimonials? (Zero unverified claims).
5. **Brand Tone & Register**: Direct response punchy, authoritative enterprise B2B, witty conversational, or polished editorial.

## Part 1: Full-Page & Web Formulas

### 1. AIDA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Action)
*The great-grandfather of copywriting formulas. Universally effective across landing pages, ads, and emails.*
- **Attention**: Hit the reader with a provocative headline, surprising statistic, or bold statement.
- **Interest**: Present fresh information, unexpected angles, or relatable scenarios that sustain curiosity.
- **Desire**: Paint the after-state. Show how their life or work transforms with the solution.
- **Action**: Direct them to take one specific, unambiguous step.

### 2. AIDCA & IDCA (Attention → Interest → Desire → Conviction → Action)
*Essential for higher-consideration B2B products where desire alone is insufficient without proof.*
- **Attention & Interest & Desire**: As in AIDA.
- **Conviction**: Deploy hard evidence—third-party benchmarks, case studies, SOC2 badges, client logos, or ROI statistics.
- **Action**: Low-friction next step.
*(IDCA variation: Omits Attention when audience is already captive, e.g., targeted email or customer portal).*

### 3. Danny Iny’s 6+1 Formula
*Clean narrative logic that bridges the gap between where the reader is and where they want to be.*
1. **Context**: Frame the situation so the reader immediately nods in recognition.
2. **Attention**: Introduce the core hook or catalyst.
3. **Desire**: Show what is possible.
4. **The Gap**: Contrast their current reality against the ideal state (the painful gap).
5. **Solution**: Introduce your product as the specific vehicle closing that gap.
6. **Call to Action**: Concrete directive.
- **+1 Credibility**: Layered consistently throughout every section (not just parked in one testimonial block).

### 4. PAS (Problem → Agitation → Solution)
*The most dependable direct-response engine in digital marketing.*
- **Problem**: Accurately define the reader's immediate frustration in their own words.
- **Agitation**: Poke the bruise. Describe the compounding cost of delay, emotional toll, or financial drain if left unsolved.
- **Solution**: Present your product as the relief valve and definitive remedy.

### 5. The 4 Ps (Two Proven Variations)
**Variation A (Emotional / Visual)**:
- **Picture**: Vividly paint a scene the reader deeply desires or desperately fears.
- **Promise**: State clearly how your product turns the positive picture into reality.
- **Prove**: Demonstrate through testimonials, data, and live demonstrations.
- **Push**: Strong CTA with urgency or a compelling reason to act now.

**Variation B (Direct B2B Proposal)**:
- **Problem**: The bottleneck holding back performance.
- **Promise**: The measurable operational upside.
- **Proof**: Technical validation, client telemetry, and security clearance.
- **Proposal**: Clear packaging, terms, and onboarding kickoff.

### 6. ACCA (Awareness → Comprehension → Conviction → Action)
*Diagnostic approach best suited for complex, innovative, or paradigm-shifting products.*
- **Awareness**: Highlight a critical situation or trend the reader might be overlooking.
- **Comprehension**: Educate them on why it happens and how it impacts their business model.
- **Conviction**: Create an unshakeable belief that your methodology is the correct approach.
- **Action**: Invite them to test, audit, or deploy the solution.

### 7. PAPA / AAPPA (Attention/Problem → Agitation/Advantage → Proof → Persuasion → Action)
*Hybrid framework combining PAS agitation with promotional persuasion.*
- **Attention / Problem**: Call out the target audience and their central challenge.
- **Advitation / Advantage**: Detail the advantages of breaking free from legacy methods.
- **Proof**: Third-party validation, user quotes, and documented deltas.
- **Persuasion**: Deep dive into features translated into high-impact advantages.
- **Action**: Friction-free signup or booking.

### 8. QUEST (Qualify → Understand → Educate → Stimulate → Transition)
*Elite consultation formula for enterprise SaaS and premium service offerings.*
- **Qualify**: Explicitly state who this is for (and who it is NOT for) to filter for high intent.
- **Understand**: Demonstrate deep empathy with their daily operational reality ("We've been in your shoes").
- **Educate**: Teach them something new about their problem or introduce a new way to frame it.
- **Stimulate**: Build excitement around the possibilities of the new model.
- **Transition**: Pivot from educator to solution provider with an actionable invitation.

### 9. SLAP (Stop → Look → Act → Purchase)
*Designed for fast, impulse, or low-cost transactional offers (e.g., templates, micro-tools, event tickets).*
- **Stop**: Interrupt pattern with arresting visual or headline.
- **Look**: Direct visual focus to the single most compelling value differentiator.
- **Act**: Trigger micro-engagement (preview, demo, calculation).
- **Purchase**: Complete checkout with single-click ease.

### 10. AIDPPC (Attention → Interest → Description → Persuasion → Proof → Close)
*Classic Robert Collier direct-mail formula adapted for comprehensive product landing pages.*
- **Attention**: Catch the reader's wandering mind.
- **Interest**: Tie your hook directly to their self-interest.
- **Description**: Provide clear, tangible details of what the product actually is and does.
- **Persuasion**: Emphasize how it alters their status, saves time, or removes stress.
- **Proof**: Iron-clad evidence, guarantees, and customer results.
- **Close**: Final deadline, pricing table, and checkout link.

### 11. 5-Point Copywriting Formula
*Addresses the 5 skeptical questions running through every buyer's subconscious mind:*
1. **What will you do for me if I listen?** (Promise & outcome upfront).
2. **How are you going to do this?** (The mechanism/system behind the promise).
3. **Who is responsible for these promises?** (Founder story, company credentials, authority).
4. **Who have you done this for?** (Customer logos, before-and-after case metrics).
5. **What will it cost me?** (Transparent pricing, time commitment, and guarantee).

### 12. DOS (Dream → Obstacle → Solution)
*Simple, human-centric framework ideal for founder-led startup copy and creator tools.*
- **Dream**: Connect with their ultimate aspiration (freedom, scale, high performance).
- **Obstacle**: Identify the single invisible bottleneck blocking them from achieving it.
- **Solution**: Position your product as the bridge that dismantles the obstacle.

### 13. AICPBSAWN
*High-converting direct-response checklist popularized by master copywriter David Raybould.*
- **A**ttention: Compelling lead.
- **I**nterest: Intriguing angle.
- **C**redibility: Why trust you.
- **P**roof: Evidence it works.
- **B**enefits: Bulleted value payoff.
- **S**carcity: Legitimate cap on availability or deadline.
- **A**ction: Unambiguous directive.
- **W**arn: Consequences of inaction.
- **N**ow: Compelling push to act right this second.

## Part 2: Long-Form Sales Pages & Direct Response

### 1. Bob Serling’s 36-Step Power Copywriting Formula
*The gold-standard architectural blueprint for multi-thousand-dollar offers and comprehensive sales pages.*
1. **Phase 1: Deep Research & Positioning** (Steps 1–4): Exhaustive product inventory, customer avatar interviews, competitor teardowns, USP lock.
2. **Phase 2: The Headline & Hook System** (Steps 5–9): Headline variants, pre-head, subhead, hook, pattern interrupt.
3. **Phase 3: The Emotional Lead & Empathy** (Steps 10–14): Connect with pain, share relatable origin story, validate that the struggle is not their fault.
4. **Phase 4: The Discovery & Mechanism** (Steps 15–18): The "Aha!" breakthrough moment, naming the proprietary mechanism.
5. **Phase 5: The Offer Stack & Proof** (Steps 19–25): The core offer, feature-benefit translation, overwhelming testimonials, third-party media citations.
6. **Phase 6: Risk Reversal & Bonuses** (Steps 26–30): Unconditional guarantee, stacked high-value bonuses, value-to-price contrast.
7. **Phase 7: The Close & Multi-P.S. Strategy** (Steps 31–36): Explicit call to action, warning of delay, P.S. (restate core benefit), P.P.S. (restate urgency/bonus deadline), P.P.P.S. (restate guarantee/risk-free trial).

### 2. Star → Story → Solution
*Timeless narrative persuasion framework.*
- **The Star**: Introduce a relatable protagonist who embodies the target customer's exact situation and desires.
- **The Story**: Follow their journey through repeated failures, mounting frustration, and the discovery of the breakthrough mechanism.
- **The Solution**: How the breakthrough was packaged into the product so others can bypass the struggle.

### 3. Bob Stone’s 7-Step Copywriting Formula
*Direct Marketing Hall of Fame blueprint.*
1. Promise your biggest benefit in your headline or first paragraph.
2. Immediately expand on this primary benefit.
3. Tell the reader exactly what they will receive (features, specifications, deliverables).
4. Back up your statements with firm proof and testimonials.
5. Remind the reader of what they stand to lose if they do not act.
6. Rephrase your primary benefit in a fresh way.
7. Stimulate urgent action with an explicit, easy next step.

### 4. Frank Egner’s 9-Point Formula
*Pioneered classic direct response book and subscription promotions.*
1. Headline that arrests attention and targets a specific audience.
2. Inspirational lead that connects on an emotional frequency.
3. Clear, concise definition of the product.
4. Success stories and customer proof points.
5. Special features and bulleted advantages.
6. Authoritative statement of value (price vs value ratio).
7. Action-stimulating offer and call to action.
8. Warning of consequences of delay.
9. Final P.S. that seals the deal.

### 5. David Frey’s 12-Step Foolproof Sales Letter
*Extremely popular framework for digital products, consulting, and SaaS demo requests.*
1. Get attention (headline).
2. Identify the problem (empathy).
3. Provide the solution (introduction).
4. Present your credentials (authority).
5. Show the benefits (bulleted payoffs).
6. Give social proof (testimonials).
7. Make your offer (the package).
8. Give a guarantee (risk reversal).
9. Inject scarcity (deadlines, cohort size).
10. Call to action (order now).
11. Give a warning (consequences of passing).
12. Close with a P.S. (reminder of urgency and reward).

### 6. Perry Belcher’s 21-Part Sales Letter Formula
*Exhaustive, high-converting structure for information products, software, and masterminds.*
1. Call out your audience.
2. Demand their attention.
3. Backup the big promise with a quick story.
4. Clarify the problem.
5. Reveal the solution.
6. Show why this is different from anything tried before.
7. List the features & translated benefits.
8. Prove your credibility & track record.
9. Show overwhelming proof & screenshots.
10. Detail the offer components.
11. Add bonuses that dissolve friction.
12. Reveal the total real-world value.
13. Reveal the actual price (anchor contrast).
14. Give a logical reason for the discount.
15. Offer a bold guarantee (100% money back or double value).
16. Inject urgency & authentic scarcity.
17. Clear Call to Action with step-by-step instructions.
18. Post-script warning.
19. Reassurance & customer quote.
20. Secondary CTA.
21. Final P.S. and closing thought.

### 7. HELLYEAH
*Punchy, aggressive direct-response framework.*
- **H**oller: Grab immediate attention with an unmistakable headline.
- **E**mpathize: Demonstrate you understand their hidden pain.
- **L**ambaste: Criticize the bad advice, legacy tools, or status quo holding them back.
- **L**egitimise: Validate why they haven't succeeded yet (it's not their fault).
- **Y**ield: Give them the missing puzzle piece / mechanism.
- **E**ducate: Walk through how the new mechanism works.
- **A**ction: Demand the click or purchase.
- **H**ound: Follow up with relentless urgency and reminders.

### 8. PASTOR
*John Ray's shepherd-inspired framework, designed to guide and protect the prospect.*
- **P**roblem: Identify the pain.
- **A**mplify: Highlight the cost of leaving it unresolved.
- **S**tory & Solution: The narrative of transformation and the vehicle.
- **T**estimonial: Real people sharing real transformations.
- **O**ffer: The complete package designed to solve the issue.
- **R**esponse: The directive inviting them to step forward.

## Part 3: Video Sales Letters (VSLs)

### 1. The Jim Edwards VSL Method
*Engineered specifically for 5-to-15 minute video sales letters with high retention and conversion.*
1. **The Snap Hook (0:00–0:30)**: Pattern interrupt, shock statement, or big bold promise.
2. **The Problem & Villain (0:30–2:00)**: Name the real enemy (bad advice, broken systems, deceptive vendors) and exonerate the viewer.
3. **The Agitation & Dark Moment (2:00–3:30)**: Describe the lowest point of dealing with this problem.
4. **The Eureka Moment (3:30–5:00)**: How the new breakthrough mechanism was discovered.
5. **The Proof & Demonstration (5:00–7:00)**: Live screen recordings, dashboards, customer video snippets, and receipts.
6. **The Transition & Offer Reveal (7:00–9:00)**: Introduce the product, its name, and everything included in the box.
7. **The Value Stacking (9:00–10:30)**: Detail bonuses, calculate total replacement value, then contrast against the real price.
8. **The 365/30-Day Guarantee (10:30–11:30)**: Zero-risk trial conditions clearly spoken.
9. **The Two Choices Close (11:30–13:00)**: Option A: Leave this page, do nothing, stay in pain. Option B: Click the button below, risk nothing, transform results.
10. **The Clock & Urgency (13:00–End)**: Countdown timer, limited onboarding slots, final push to click.

### 2. Common 3-CTA Video Blueprint
*Paces multiple calls-to-action throughout the video to capture different viewer commitment thresholds.*
- **First 25%**: Hook → Story → Introduce Solution → **CTA #1 (Early Adopters / Eager Buyers)**.
- **Middle 50%**: Deep dive into mechanics, feature-to-benefit demos, case studies, bonus reveals → **CTA #2 (Analytical Buyers)**.
- **Final 25%**: Address FAQs, dismantle skeptical objections, present guarantee, issue warning of inaction → **CTA #3 (Skeptical / Last-Chance Buyers)**.

## Part 4: Headlines & Value Propositions

### Value Proposition Frameworks

1. **Geoffrey Moore’s Positioning Statement**:
   > For `[target customer]` who `[statement of need/opportunity]`, our `[product name]` is a `[product category]` that `[statement of key benefit]`. Unlike `[primary alternative]`, our product `[statement of primary differentiation]`.

2. **Steve Blank’s VAD (Value-Action-Differentiation)**:
   > We help `[X]` do `[Y]` by doing `[Z]`.  
   *(e.g., "We help agency founders double client retainers by automating technical SEO audits.")*

3. **Venture Hacks High-Concept Pitch ("X for Y")**:
   > The `[Well-Known Success]` for `[New Industry / Niche]`.  
   *(e.g., "Figma for database schemas", "Shopify for cloud AI workflows").*

4. **Dave McClure’s Elevator Pitch (What, How, Why)**:
   - What we do (in 5 words or less).
   - How we do it (the unique mechanism).
   - Why it matters (the financial or emotional transformation).

5. **Customer-Problem-Solution Framework**:
   - The Customer: Specific role and company stage.
   - The Burning Problem: The expensive friction they face today.
   - The Non-Obvious Solution: Why standard approaches fail and our method wins.

## Part 5: Bullet Lists & Fascinations

Bulleted copy must never be a dry grocery list of software specifications. Every bullet must act as a mini-headline designed to provoke intense curiosity, promise a tangible payoff, or tease a non-obvious revelation.

### BGNGo (Benefit, Goody, Name, Go)
- **Benefit**: What will this do for their bottom line or peace of mind?
- **Goody**: What is the exact deliverable or artifact included?
- **Name**: Give the feature or concept a proprietary, memorable name.
- **Go**: Connect it directly to immediate application.

### The 7 Deadly Fascinations
1. **The "How-To" Fascination**:
   - *"How to slash database query latency by 80% with a single 3-line Redis index configuration."*
2. **The "Secret" Fascination**:
   - *"The secret reason Why 70% of enterprise signups abandon at step 2—and the 1-field fix that recaptures them."*
3. **The "Number / Specific Metric" Fascination**:
   - *"The exact 7-word phrase that boosted demo attendance from 52% to 89% in cold B2B campaigns."*
4. **The "Warning / Trap" Fascination**:
   - *"Warning: Never run this single npm command on production Docker containers unless you want your memory to leak."*
5. **The "Truth About" Fascination**:
   - *"The truth about AI code generators: why most benchmarks lie, and how to accurately measure true developer velocity."*
6. **The "Paradox / Contradiction" Fascination**:
   - *"Why adding MORE fields to your checkout form can actually INCREASE enterprise conversion rates."*
7. **The "Quick / Effortless" Fascination**:
   - *"The 2-minute morning routine top engineering leads use to eliminate slack interruptions before noon."*

## Part 6: Body Copy & Micro-Persuasion

### FAB (Feature → Advantage → Benefit)
Never leave a feature naked. Walk the triple-step ladder:
- **Feature**: What it is (technical specification).
- **Advantage**: What it does (operational capability).
- **Benefit**: What it means for the human user (time saved, money earned, status elevated, stress removed).
*(Example: Feature: 1-click Postgres rollbacks → Advantage: Instantly restore state to any minute in the last 30 days → Benefit: Sleep soundly during midnight migrations knowing a bad script won't destroy client data).*

### The "So What?" Rule
After every single sentence written in body copy, ask: *"So what? Why should my busy reader care?"* If the answer isn't immediately obvious in the text, delete or rewrite it.

## Part 7: High-Converting CTAs & Testimonial Architecture

### Call to Action (CTA) Engineering

1. **The First-Person "I Want" Rule**:
   - Complete the sentence: *"I want to..."*
   - Bad: `Submit`, `Click Here`, `Learn More`
   - Good: `Get My Free Audit`, `Claim My Seat`, `Start Building for Free`, `Double My Conversions`

2. **RAD Framework (Require → Acquire → Desire)**:
   - **Require**: What does the user require before clicking? (Clear answer to: What does it cost? How long does it take? Is a credit card required?).
   - **Acquire**: What will they physically or digitally acquire the instant they click?
   - **Desire**: What emotional or professional desire does this satisfy?

3. **The Value CTA Triple**:
   `[Action Verb]` + `[Tangible Get]` + `[Risk Reducer / Qualifier]`
   *(e.g., "Start Free — Get Instant Benchmark Report — No Credit Card, 60 Seconds")*

4. **Hook, Line, and Sinker**:
   - **Hook**: Benefit headline immediately preceding the button.
   - **Line**: The button itself.
   - **Sinker**: Microcopy immediately below the button that diffuses fear (e.g., *"Takes 2 minutes. Cancel anytime with 1 click. Zero spam policy."*).

---

### Testimonial Architecture & Social Proof

Raw quotes like *"Great software, highly recommend!"* are useless. High-converting testimonials showcase specific transformations and dissolve hesitations.

1. **Before → During → After (The Transformation Arc)**:
   - **Before**: What specific frustration, hesitation, or bottleneck existed?
   - **During**: How frictionless and straightforward was the setup/implementation?
   - **After**: What exact numerical or qualitative breakthrough occurred?

2. **TEASE Framework**:
   - **T**estimonial: The direct authentic quote.
   - **E**vidence: Screenshot, dashboard metric, or live URL.
   - **A**uthority: Full name, title, company logo, and verified headshot.
   - **S**pecificity: Exact numbers, timeframe, and concrete details.
   - **E**motion: How the customer felt once the problem was resolved.

3. **The 4 S's**:
   - **Specific**: Contains real metrics ($24,000 saved, 14 hours shaved off weekly).
   - **Short**: Edited down to 2–3 punchy sentences (bolding key phrases).
   - **Sincere**: Preserves authentic colloquial voice, not corporate PR speak.
   - **Solution-focused**: Highlights the specific feature that made the difference.

4. **The 6-Question Reverse Testimonial Engine**:
   When surveying customers for quotes, ask these exact questions to extract perfect copy:
   1. *What was your biggest hesitation before buying/signing up?*
   2. *What happened when you actually started using the product?*
   3. *What specific feature or aspect surprised you the most?*
   4. *What measurable results or changes have you experienced since?*
   5. *Would you recommend this to peers, and why?*
   6. *Is there anything else you'd like to add?*

## Part 8: Email Sequences & Subject Lines

### Battle-Tested Email Sequences

1. **Rob Walling’s 5-Day SaaS Onboarding Drip**:
   - **Day 1**: Welcome + The single fastest path to "First Value" (quick win).
   - **Day 2**: The most underutilized, high-ROI feature that pro users love.
   - **Day 3**: Case study / customer success story illustrating 10x ROI.
   - **Day 4**: Common mistake teardown (how to avoid doing it the hard way).
   - **Day 5**: Personal invitation to ask questions / upgrade offer / concierge check-in.

2. **Wishpond 5-Part Lead Nurture Sequence**:
   - **Email 1**: Deliver the promised resource + set expectations.
   - **Email 2**: Share an origin story / the philosophy behind why your approach is different.
   - **Email 3**: Introduce the core problem and agitate the compounding cost of delay.
   - **Email 4**: Comprehensive breakdown of the solution with client proof.
   - **Email 5**: Hard offer with clear risk reversal and deadline.

3. **String of Pearls**:
   - A sequence of short, standalone emails where every single email delivers one distinct, valuable "pearl" of wisdom, data, or practical advice. Each ends with a casual P.S. pointing to the main offer.

4. **PASOP Email Sequence (Problem → Agitate → Solution → Offer → Push)**:
   - 5-part campaign where each email focuses on one stage of the PASOP progression.

5. **6-Email Product / Feature Launch Sequence**:
   - **Email 1 (Teaser / The Rumor)**: Something new is coming.
   - **Email 2 (The Problem)**: Why current tools fail and what needs to change.
   - **Email 3 (The Big Reveal)**: Product announcement, demo video, early-bird access opens.
   - **Email 4 (Social Proof & Case Study)**: Early user reactions and live results.
   - **Email 5 (Objection Crusher & FAQ)**: Addressing top 5 questions head-on.
   - **Email 6 (Final Call / Cart Close)**: Urgency, expiring bonuses, final warning.

## Part 9: Paid Ads & Social Copy

### Paid Search Ads (Google / Bing)
- **AIU (Attention, Interest, Urgency)**:
  - Headline 1: Exact keyword match (Attention).
  - Headline 2: High-impact benefit / proprietary differentiator (Interest).
  - Headline 3 / Description: Clear risk-reversal and deadline / call to action (Urgency).
- **Wordstream 4-Part Structure**:
  - Part 1: Pain point / query acknowledgment.
  - Part 2: Product solution.
  - Part 3: Social proof metric / badge.
  - Part 4: Direct call to action with value proposition.

### Paid Social Ads (Meta / LinkedIn / TikTok)
- **ERERS Framework**:
  - **E**motion: Disrupt the scroll with relatable frustration or arresting visual.
  - **R**eason: Provide logical justification for why standard methods fall short.
  - **E**mpathy: Validate their struggle ("It’s not because your team lacks talent").
  - **R**esponse: Show what happens when using the new methodology.
  - **S**ocial Proof: Conclude with hard client metrics and testimonial snippet.
- **Facebook 4-Step Ad**:
  1. Call out the audience directly.
  2. Ask a leading question that forces a "Yes!" nod.
  3. Introduce the solution in one tight sentence.
  4. CTA link with compelling bonus.

### Social Media & Twitter/X Hooks
- **The Contrarian Hook**: `Everyone tells you to do [X]. After scaling to [Outcome], I do the exact opposite. Here's why:`
- **The Teardown Hook**: `I spent 40 hours analyzing [Brand/Product]'s conversion funnel. Here are the 5 genius tactics they use to print revenue:`

## Part 10: Pre-Publishing Audit & Quality Verification Gates

Before publishing or marking any copy ready, pass it through these 5 rigorous editorial filters:

### 1. "So What? Prove It." Audit
- Go line by line. If a sentence makes a claim (*"Our platform is lightning fast"*), ask:
  1. *So what?* → *"It saves engineers 4 hours a week compiling."*
  2. *Prove it!* → *"Benchmarked on 5,000 Next.js repos, slashing p95 build times from 12m to 42s."*
- If you cannot prove it with a real number or fact, delete the claim.

### 2. The 4 Cs
- **Clear**: Can a tired reader grasp the message in 3 seconds?
- **Concise**: Have all filler words, throat-clearing leads, and redundant adverbs been purged?
- **Compelling**: Does the reader feel a genuine pull toward the outcome?
- **Credible**: Are all claims supported by verifiable proof points?

### 3. The 4 Us
- **Useful**: Does this copy deliver immediate practical value to the reader?
- **Urgent**: Is there a legitimate reason to act today rather than bookmark for later?
- **Unique**: Does this present an angle or mechanism they haven't seen 100 times before?
- **Ultra-specific**: Are generic words replaced with exact numbers, names, and concrete tools?

### 4. A FOREST (Rhetorical Persuasion Check)
- **A**lliteration: Memorable, rhythmic phrasing for key slogans.
- **F**acts: Concrete observed truths.
- **O**pinions: Strong, authoritative brand stance.
- **R**epetition: Re-emphasizing the core promise across multiple angles.
- **E**xamples: Specific scenarios demonstrating product utility.
- **S**tatistics: Hard percentages and deltas.
- **T**hrees: Rule of three rhythm (`Faster. Safer. Simpler.`).

### 5. Aaron 8 Pre-Flight Auditor Gates
*Mandatory quality standard before sign-off (source: `aaron-he-zhu/aaron-marketing-skills`):*
1. **CORE-EEAT (Quality & Authority Gate)**:
   - *CORE*: Contextual Clarity (purpose unmistakable in 5 seconds), Organization (logical hierarchy), Referenceability (modular, cite-friendly layout), Exclusivity (first-party data, proprietary angle, or novel insight).
   - *EEAT*: Experience (first-person evidence), Expertise (technical domain depth), Authoritativeness (named credentials/case evidence), Trust (transparent commercial intent, zero unverifiable claims).
2. **CITE (Algorithmic Quotability Gate)**:
   - *18-Token Self-Contained Rule*: Key claims and definitions must be extractable as complete standalone sentences without dangling pronouns (`this`, `they`, `these`).
   - *Primary Source Linkage*: All statistical proof points must cite primary empirical studies or first-party benchmarks, never secondary blog aggregators.
3. **STAR (Case Study & Proof Point Gate)**:
   - Enforce Situation → Task → Action → Result for B2B evidence.
   - *Result Metric Mandate*: Prohibit vague claims ("greatly accelerated onboarding"); mandate baseline, timeframe, and quantified delta ("slashed P99 onboarding latency from 14 days to 4 hours over 60 days").
4. **ROAS (Acquisition Efficiency Gate)**:
   - Hook and angle audit: ensure the headline promise aligns directly with post-click destination to prevent bounce and wasted ad spend.
5. **SEND (Outreach & Email Copy Gate)**:
   - Subject line clarity, Emotional resonance, Novelty angle, and deliverability check before dispatch.
6. **RAMP (Readiness Gate)**:
   - Relevance, Authority, Mechanics (syntax, mobile line breaks), and Polish.
7. **ECHO (Tonal Integrity Gate)**:
   - Voice consistency verification against approved brand persona vectors — zero abrupt register shifts.
8. **TALE (Narrative Arc Gate)**:
   - Tension → Adversity → Lesson → Elevation sequence to maintain cognitive engagement over flat, bulleted recitation.
